How Phishing Attacks Work

Phishing is one of the most common ways cybercriminals steal information. An attacker sends you an email that looks like it comes from a trusted source — your bank, your payment app, your employer — and tricks you into clicking a link and entering your credentials.

The email you just clicked was a simulated phishing attack designed to look like a real message from a trusted Nigerian brand. You entered your credentials on a fake page — in a real attack, those credentials would now belong to a criminal.

🚩 Red Flags In That Email

The sender domain was not the official company domain — real companies always send from their official domain.

The email created urgency — "your account has been restricted" — to make you act without thinking.

The link did not go to the real website — always check the URL before entering any information.

How To Protect Yourself

  • Always check the sender's email address carefully — fake domains often look similar to real ones
  • Hover over links before clicking to see the real destination URL
  • When in doubt, go directly to the website by typing it in your browser — never click links in emails
  • Legitimate companies will never ask for your password via email
  • If you receive a suspicious email at work, report it to your IT team immediately
  • Enable two-factor authentication on all important accounts
